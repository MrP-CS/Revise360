Using Revise 360 in the classroom

Revise 360 turns GCSE Computer Science revision into 360° rooms that students explore, on a laptop, a tablet or in a VR headset.

What a lesson looks like

  1. Before the headsets: students answer the starter on the worksheet. Five minutes, no devices.
  2. In the experience: six numbered stations plus a starred final challenge. At each station they read the wall, write the key fact and the challenge answer on the worksheet, then tap the badge to answer the questions.
  3. Blue i markers hold extra facts. They're not assessed, and they're deliberately interesting: good bait for early finishers.
  4. The score screen shows a breakdown by station, marked Secure, Revise or Focus here. Students copy the score onto the worksheet.
  5. After: the worksheet's exam practice is done with the headset off, from memory.

A full experience takes 25 to 40 minutes. Review mode lets students retry only the questions they got wrong, without changing their recorded score, which makes it useful as a starter the following lesson.

Getting the worksheets right

  • Print them. The design assumes writing while wearing or holding a device, so boxes are large and the order matches the stations.
  • Insist on writing before tapping. The questions are easy to guess; the worksheet is where the thinking happens.
  • The final challenge has a prediction table. Students commit to an answer on paper before the interactive version marks it.
  • Exam practice is the plenary. Mark it, or peer-mark it, using the answer sheets.
  • Revision lessons include a RAG checklist and a results table to plan what to revise.

VR mode

  • Works in any headset with a WebXR browser, including Meta Quest 2, 3 and 3S. Nothing to install: open the site in the headset's browser and press Enter VR.
  • Students sign in on the flat screen first, then enter VR. Point with a controller and pull the trigger; hand tracking works with a pinch.
  • Look down for the ☰ menu: score, progress, review mode and Exit VR. The thumbstick turns the view for seated use.
  • Interactive boards, such as building a logic circuit or painting pixels, work in VR too: hold the trigger to drag.
  • For ClassVR and other headsets without WebXR, the 360° images can be uploaded as standalone panoramas; the quizzes then happen on the worksheet.
  • No headsets? Everything works with a mouse or a touchscreen, and nothing is lost except the immersion.

Tracking progress

Progress saves automatically in the student's browser as they answer. A teacher dashboard shows scores by class, the weakest stations across a group, and a CSV download.

A hosted version, where you can create student accounts and see progress across devices without sharing personal data, is in development. Until then the dashboard shows students who have used that device or browser.

Answer sheets

Each worksheet has a matching answer sheet with model answers, the correct response to every question in the experience, and marking notes: what earns the mark, what to accept, and the mistakes to watch for. These are supplied to registered teachers rather than published here, so students can't find them.

To request them, email hello@revise360.co.uk from your school address.
