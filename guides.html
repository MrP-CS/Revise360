How to use Revise 360

Two guides: one for students, one for teachers. Both take about five minutes to read, and there's a troubleshooting section at the bottom for the things that usually go wrong.

Student guide Teacher guide VR headsets Troubleshooting

For students

  1. Open a topic and pick an experience. There is no sign-in at the moment: your progress is saved in the browser you are using.
  2. Pick a topic, then an experience. Each card says how many stations it has and whether there's a worksheet to print.
  3. Look around. Drag with the mouse, swipe on a tablet, or use the arrow keys. There's a numbered badge at each station and blue i markers with extra facts.
  4. Read the wall first, then write. Put the key fact and your challenge answer on the worksheet before you tap the badge. The questions are easy to guess; the writing is where the learning happens.
  5. Answer the questions. Your first answer counts, so think before you tap. Feedback appears straight away.
  6. Do the final challenge. Look down for the star. Predict the answer on the worksheet first.
  7. Check your score. You get a breakdown by station: Secure, Revise, or Focus here. Copy it onto the worksheet, and use Review mode to retry anything you got wrong. Review mode never changes your recorded score.
  8. Finish the worksheet. Device away, exam practice from memory. That's the part that sticks.

Where your progress lives. It is saved in this browser only. Use the same computer to carry on, and copy your scores onto the worksheet so you keep them.

Using a VR headset

  1. Open the site in the headset's browser and sign in as normal on the flat screen.
  2. Open an experience, then press 🥽 Enter VR in the toolbar. If the button isn't there, the headset's browser doesn't support VR: everything still works on the flat screen.
  3. Point and pull the trigger to choose. With hand tracking, point and pinch instead.
  4. Look down for the ☰ menu: your score, progress, review mode, the scene switcher and Exit VR.
  5. Turn with the thumbstick if you're sitting down, rather than twisting in the chair.
  6. Interactive boards work too. Hold the trigger to drag a gate, a tile or a pixel; release to drop it.
  7. Take a break every 20 minutes, and stop if you feel unwell. Stay seated unless your teacher says otherwise.

For teachers

  1. Open a topic from the topics page and pick an experience. Nothing to set up and no accounts while the site is in development.
  2. Print the worksheets. Every experience has one, linked from its card and from the toolbar inside the experience.
  3. Run the lesson. Starter on paper, then the six stations, then the final challenge, then exam practice with the device away. A full experience takes 25 to 40 minutes.
  4. Watch the room, not the screens. Insist students write before tapping. That single rule is the difference between revision and a quiz game.
  5. Use the score screen. Each student sees a breakdown by station, marked Secure, Revise or Focus here. Ask them to copy it onto the worksheet, since progress is stored only in their own browser for now.
  6. Answer sheets are for teachers: email hello@revise360.co.uk from your school address.

Getting a set of headsets going

  • Open the site on every headset before the lesson and leave it on the topic page.
  • Agree a signal for "headsets down": a hand in the air works better than shouting.
  • Have spare printed worksheets. A student without a headset can do the whole lesson on paper and a laptop.

If something goes wrong

"Offline: saved on this device"

The internet dropped or the site can't reach the server. Work is safe. It syncs when the connection returns, or when the tab is reopened.

The Enter VR button is missing

That browser doesn't support WebXR. Use the headset's own browser, or run the experience on the flat screen: no content is lost.

A student's score looks too low

The first answer counts. Review mode lets them retry without changing the record, so the score reflects what they knew at the time, which is the point.

Is any data collected?

No. Progress is stored in your own browser and nothing is sent anywhere. Accounts will return with Microsoft sign-in, and the privacy page will be updated before that happens.
