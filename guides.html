How to use Revise 360

Use the lesson slides, worksheet and 360° experience together, from the starter to the plenary.

For students

  1. Get ready. Follow your teacher's sign-in instructions and choose the topic and lesson. Download the worksheet and, where listed, the Lesson PowerPoint from the experience card.
  2. Complete the starter. Attempt the question before viewing its answer slide. Mark and improve your response.
  3. Check the key terms. Try the quiz, then use the following definitions to correct your answers.
  4. Open the experience when instructed. Read the task slide first. Drag with the mouse or swipe to look around; use the numbered badges to open station questions.
  5. Write in your own words. Read the information, explain the key facts on your worksheet and record your challenge answer before checking it in the experience.
  6. Complete the challenge and review. Follow the lesson's final challenge, record your score and revisit mistakes in Review mode. Your first-attempt score stays the same.
  7. Finish with the plenary. Close the experience and answer the worksheet's knowledge checks from memory. Then use the PowerPoint's model answers to mark and improve your work.

Keep your work. In the current local demo, progress and logins belong to the browser you are using. Keep your completed worksheet; changing devices or clearing browser data will not transfer your progress.

For teachers

  1. Choose and prepare. Open the experience selection screen, download the Lesson PowerPoint and Worksheet where listed, and preview both. Print the worksheet or make it available for students to complete.
  2. Mark as you go. The starter answers follow the starter, and key-term answers follow the terminology quiz. Allow thinking time before revealing each answer slide.
  3. Display the task instructions. Use the combined worksheet and experience slide as the board instructions. Emphasise key facts in students' own words and say when to open the experience.
  4. Support completion. Plan time for all students to finish the experience and move to the plenary towards the end of the lesson. Students ready to move on can improve explanations or review errors.
  5. Check understanding. Use the plenary model answers for self-marking, peer-marking or discussion. The slides are downloadable by students too, so keep unseen assessments separate.

Lesson PowerPoints are being added across the course. Downloads are currently available for Topics 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3; each experience card shows the resources available for that lesson. Standalone games and paper assessments may use a different format.

To test the dashboard, use the key demo at teacher sign-in and create fictional student logins. These only work in the same browser. Microsoft sign-in and cross-device class tracking are not yet enabled.

Homework and intervention

Working at home: follow the worksheet section or lesson slides your teacher has assigned. Write explanations in your own words and attempt questions before looking at the answers. Return the completed worksheet through your teacher's usual homework system.

In an intervention session: focus on the stations or key terms you need to strengthen. Discuss your reasoning with your teacher, correct misconceptions and finish with a short knowledge check. You can use the resources in a small group or one-to-one, without a VR headset.

The current demo saves logins and scores in one browser only. Use downloaded slides and worksheets for home study if your classroom login is not available on your home device.

Using a VR headset

  1. Check the device first. Open the site in a headset browser that supports WebXR. Try the lesson before classroom use.
  2. Open the experience on the flat screen, then choose Enter VR when available.
  3. Point and select. Use a controller trigger; hand-tracking support depends on the device and browser.
  4. Look down for the menu. Use the score, progress, review and Exit VR controls. Interactive boards support controller dragging where available.
  5. Pause for written work. Remove the headset for writing and whole-class marking. Follow the manufacturer's guidance and your teacher's instructions on breaks and safe use.

No headset is needed: the lesson resources and experiences can be used on a PC or tablet.

If something goes wrong

I cannot find a Lesson PowerPoint button

PowerPoints are currently published for Topics 1.1–1.3. A card only shows a PowerPoint button when a deck is available; other topics and standalone activities may not have one yet.

The PowerPoint downloads rather than opening

Open the downloaded .pptx file in PowerPoint or another compatible presentation app. In a school-managed browser, ask IT if downloads are blocked.

Where are the answers?

Starter answers and key-term definitions follow their questions. Plenary model answers appear later in the lesson deck. Attempt each task before revealing the answers.

My progress is missing on another device

The current demo stores progress in one browser only. Return to the original browser, or use your worksheet to keep track. Signing out does not erase all stored work; clearing the site's browser data does.

The internet connection drops

Keep the current tab open. Loaded activities may continue, but new rooms, downloads and other assets can still need a connection. Local progress is not a cloud backup.

The Enter VR button is missing

Check the headset's browser and WebXR support. You can still use the experience on the flat screen.

A score does not change after a correction

Review mode records corrections without replacing the original first-attempt score.

What data is stored?

Local demo logins, class lists and progress are saved in the browser. Hosting and external assets also involve web requests. Read the privacy page for details.
