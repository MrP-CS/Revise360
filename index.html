THE PYTHON COURSE

From your first print()
to independent programs.

Thirteen lessons that start from nothing. A student who has never written a line of code writes one in the first minute, and by lesson thirteen is writing whole programs that read a file, search a list and answer an exam question.

Every program runs in the browser. Nothing to install, no account to set up on a laptop that already has one.

  1. 01

    See a small example

    One idea, three lines, and a sentence saying what it does.

  2. 02

    Run it

    Press Run and watch what happens. Nothing is marked: the point is to see it work.

  3. 03

    Say what it will do

    Predict the output before running it. This is where a misreading surfaces early, while it is still cheap to put right.

  4. 04

    Change one thing

    Then finish a program with a gap in it, then find a real error in a broken one.

  5. 05

    Build it yourself

    From nothing, with a brief. Press Check and your program is run against its test cases, so the mark is what it does, not how it looks.

  6. 06

    Get told what to fix

    A failing test shows what was expected and what came out. The hint is a ladder you climb a rung at a time, and the top of it is still the technique on different data, never the answer.

BUILT FOR LEARNING, NOT JUST REVISION

Every principle is a feature you can point at.

Retrieval

Every worksheet opens with a starter about the lesson before it, answered on paper and corrected before anything else happens. Review mode reopens the questions a student got wrong, not the whole lesson again.

Worked examples

The early activities of a station carry a worked example with its own data, and the later ones carry none. The support fades on purpose.

Guided practice

Run it, predict it, change one thing, fill the gap, fix the error — then write it from nothing. Six steps, in that order, every station.

Checking

Every multiple-choice question offers wrong answers that are real misconceptions, not filler, so the split across the options tells a teacher what to reteach. The score screen breaks down by station, not into one number.

Independent application

The hardest activity on each station comes last and is not optional, and the worksheet carries exam-style questions to answer away from the screen.

Nothing is skipped

In the Python course a question opens when the one before it is finished. A student who is stuck gets the hint, then asks their teacher. There is no way past a question except through it.

LESSONS, HOMEWORK & INTERVENTION

Three things that fit together.

The slides are for the front of the room: the key question, the starter, key terms and the task. The experience is where a student reads, decides and is marked. The worksheet is where they write the key fact in their own words and answer exam-style questions, and it is what survives when the devices do not.

Download the slides and worksheet from any experience card, or take a whole unit at once from the teacher dashboard: one download with every PowerPoint and worksheet in it, and a second with the unit's pedagogy, its specification map, a delivery guide and a lesson plan for every lesson.

PowerPoints, worksheets and teacher guides are downloadable. The interactive 360° experiences are used on this website and are not part of any download.
