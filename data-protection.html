Data protection policy

This is the full policy for schools and data protection leads. It explains what Revise 360 processes, why, where it is kept, for how long, and what a school can ask for. The short version is on the privacy page.

Version 1.0 · September 2026 · Applies to revise360.co.uk and its backend service

The principle behind the design. Revise 360 is used by children, so it is built to hold as little as possible. It never asks for a name, an email address, a date of birth or any contact detail, and a PIN never leaves the device in a readable form. A record on its own does not identify a child: it identifies a username that only the school can resolve.

A written processor agreement under Article 28 of the UK GDPR is provided to every subscribing school before any pupil data is processed. Email hello@revise360.co.uk for a copy to review in advance.

No special category data is processed. No profiling and no automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects takes place: scores are feedback on quiz answers, nothing more.

3. What is never processed

  • Real names, email addresses, dates of birth, addresses or phone numbers
  • PINs or passwords in readable form: the PIN is combined with the username and class in the browser and only the resulting one-way code is sent
  • Location, microphone, camera, or headset sensor data of any kind
  • Advertising identifiers, third-party cookies or analytics trackers. There is no advertising on the service, and data is never sold, rented or shared for marketing

4. Where data is held, and transfers

Progress is stored in the browser's local storage on the device, and in a Cloudflare D1 database hosted in the UK and the European Union. Cloudflare may route requests through its global network, and any resulting transfer outside the UK is covered by the UK International Data Transfer Addendum and Standard Contractual Clauses under Cloudflare's own data processing addendum.

6. Security

  • All traffic is encrypted in transit with HTTPS, and data at rest is encrypted by the hosting platform.
  • PINs are never stored. The sign-in code is a one-way hash and cannot be reversed to recover a PIN.
  • Dashboard access requires a teacher key, checked in a way that resists guessing by timing. Each school has its own key, and keys are never shared between schools.
  • Requests are size-limited and validated, and the database is reached only through parameterised queries.
  • Access to production systems is limited to the service owner, protected by multi-factor authentication.
  • The system is designed so that a compromise would expose usernames and quiz scores, not identities or credentials.

7. Pupils' rights

Requests are made to the school as controller, which can act directly in the dashboard or ask Revise 360 to act. Because records contain no contact details, a school must identify the username concerned.

  • Access: an export of that username's records, provided within one calendar month.
  • Rectification: a username or class label can be corrected.
  • Erasure: all records for a username deleted, normally the same working day.
  • Objection and restriction: a school can switch off syncing so nothing leaves the device.
  • Portability: records are exported as CSV or JSON.

8. If something goes wrong

Any suspected personal data breach is investigated immediately, and affected schools are told without undue delay and in any case within 24 hours of confirmation, with what happened, what data was affected, what has been done and what the school should consider. As processor, Revise 360 supports the school in any report it must make to the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours.

9. Children

The service is designed for supervised classroom use with 11 to 16 year olds. It has no chat, no messaging, no user-generated content, no public leaderboards, no external links inside the experiences and no way for students to contact one another. Nothing a student writes is published anywhere. Schools should include Revise 360 in the privacy notice they give to parents; a short paragraph is available on request.

10. Helping with your DPIA

Most schools find this service low risk, but the following is provided on request to support a data protection impact assessment: the processor agreement, a data flow description, the retention schedule, details of sub-processors, security measures, and a completed supplier questionnaire.

11. Changes and contact

Material changes to this policy are notified to subscribing schools by email at least 30 days in advance. Questions, requests, agreements and breach notifications: hello@revise360.co.uk.

If you are unhappy with how data has been handled, you may complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Schools running their own copy. If your school hosts its own backend, none of the pupil data reaches Revise 360 at all. In that case you are both controller and processor for the progress data, and this policy applies only to the site's own content.
